### 1. Add one line telling every agent to disagree when a request conflicts with your goals
[18 Tips to Set Up Your Ultimate AI Second Brain for 2027](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pXHpUyC54uQ) (Ben AI, Oct 28, 30 min, transcript read; tip 18 at 27:56)

**What it is:** Ben says AI tools tend to agree with whoever is talking to them, so he adds one line to the shared agents file: "always act in the best interest of the business based on our goals and our strategy. If a request or output conflicts with that, say so clearly instead of just agreeing." He cites an a16z piece on institutional versus individual AI (not read).
**Why it matters:** Your global rules cover tone and structure but nothing tells Claude to push back when a request cuts against a decision you already made (checked `claude/CLAUDE.md`; only "Reversals" restates a design after you override it). Whether one line changes behavior is Ben's claim, not tested (inferred fit).
**How to try it:** Add the line to `claude/CLAUDE.md` in your own words, for example: "If a request conflicts with a decision or goal Matt already stated, say so in one sentence before doing it." Then run `node install.js` if that is how the file is deployed, and watch the next few sessions.
**Suggested change:** `claude/CLAUDE.md`, new two-line section after "Reversals and long sessions". Your call on wording.
**Client angle:** For a client's shared instructions file, one line pointing the AI at the business's stated goals keeps it from simply agreeing with each team member.

## Worth knowing

### Before building a Claude dashboard, name the decision it serves
[Claude's new DASHBOARDS Are Amazing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3BkoIxN-EXs) (Ben AI, Nov 1, 12 min, transcript read)

**What it is:** A five-step method for Claude's new live dashboards: set a goal, pick 5 to 10 metrics, check each number can be pulled, choose a layout from reference screenshots, then build.
**How to try it:** Start the chat with: "I want a dashboard for [topic]. Interview me one question at a time until you can write its goal in one sentence: the one to three decisions it should help me make." Then ask Claude to list how each metric is calculated, its time period, and whether it loads live or needs a scheduled routine. Ben says dashboards need a routine for AI-written insights.
**Why it is worth knowing:** The decision-first rule is the part that keeps a dashboard used; Ben's claims about limits (100 MB database) and shared viewers needing their own connectors are his, unverified.
