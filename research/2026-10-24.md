## Kept items

### 1. Pick the effort level by task type, not "higher is better"
[Anthropic Just Revealed Which Effort Level to Use](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q876SRB_-1M) (Ben AI, Oct 22, 12 min, transcript read)

**What it is:** A walkthrough of an Anthropic article on effort levels. Low for iterating together, simple tasks and polishing; medium (the Opus 5.5 default) for most work; high where a hidden mistake costs money or trust, or for a final verification pass; extra high or max only for long, hard, critical jobs run unattended.
**Why it matters:** Ben reports Anthropic's numbers: the same simple redesign took 1 minute on low and 28 minutes on max with a similar result, and max can add cost for small gains or overthinking. His summary also says switching effort mid-chat no longer re-reads the conversation at full price on the newest models (claimed in the video, unverified). Your Token cost rule says "cheapest model and effort that can still do that subtask" but gives no guidance on which effort fits which subtask (inferred gap).
**Suggested change:** `claude/CLAUDE.md`, "Token cost" step 2: add one line mapping effort to subtask type (low for retrieval, tagging, quick drafts; medium default; high for checking numbers or verifying a finished piece; xhigh/max only for long unattended critical jobs). Note the Agent tool only takes an effort value when you or CLAUDE.md ask for one, so this line is what would authorize it.
**Client angle:** Build the first version at low, then run one high pass to verify numbers against the source.

### 2. Trim what loads in every chat: unused skills, plugins and connectors
[Paste This Into Claude, Never Hit a Token Limit Again](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jr-jyTL2MYI) (Ben AI, Sep 14, 17 min, transcript read; outside the 8 day window, found in the addendum screen)

**What it is:** Six token-saving habits. The one not already in your rules: a fresh Claude chat spends tokens before any prompt (he shows 84,000), because every connector, plugin and skill description is loaded. His fix is to disable the ones you are not using, in the customize tab.
**Why it matters:** Ben says `/context` shows the breakdown, and plugins load all their inner skills too. This session's skill list alone has about 25 entries, including xlsx, pptx, pdf, docx, chrome-browser and computer-use, which may be unused for most of your work (inferred; I can't see your usage).
**Suggested change:** Setup change, not a repo file: run `/context` in a normal cloud session, then disable the plugin skills and connectors you don't use. If you want it recorded, add a one-line note under "Token cost" in `claude/CLAUDE.md`. Your call on which to disable.
**Client angle:** Audit which connectors and skills each person's setup loads by default before blaming the model for cost.

## Worth knowing

### Eight adoption practices from Anthropic's customer case studies
[Anthropic Just Revealed How AI-First Companies Actually Use AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jl4-8nHdH4) (Ben AI, Sep 16, 15 min, transcript read)

**What it is:** Ben's summary of Anthropic's case studies: managers adopt AI first, let power users emerge instead of appointing champions, put AI in the tools people already use, connect it to company software, keep a shared context folder, automate work nobody was doing, have process owners build their own skills, and share skills as a team asset.
**Why it matters:** The figures (Workato adoption up more than 700% after connecting its tools through MCP, a Whisk project done in 20 hours that sat in the backlog for 2 years) are Ben's reading of the case studies; I did not check the originals. The advice is the same pattern you already use with this repo as shared context.

### Opus 5.5 vs Fable 5.1 on seven real tasks
[I Tested Opus 5.5 vs Fable 5.1 on 7 Real Use Cases](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3ogITvjOh30) (Ben AI, Sep 24, 17 min, transcript read)

**What it is:** One tester's side-by-side runs with cost and time per task.
**Why it matters:** His result: Opus 5.5 was cheaper and cleaner for slide decks, design, documents and copy. Fable 5.1 went deeper on data-heavy work (it read 555 support calls to Opus's 248, and the report was better), at higher cost ($100 vs $62 on that run). One person's runs, not a benchmark. He also repeats an unverified theory that labs lower a model's effort setting a few weeks after launch.
